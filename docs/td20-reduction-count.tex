\begin{abstract}
The active route uses two transpositions producing three fixed symbols, followed
by a transposition producing at least two more, on either endpoint of a
cyclic-target pair. Domains have 21 symbols and root budget 11.
Unselected roots are generated directly. The endpoint cover consists of all
143 fixed-point-free even annotated types on 16 symbols, with budget
8. The checker independently regenerates roots, endpoints, and all inverse
waves, checks elementary final-root closure families, and replays intrinsic
sorting certificates for the remaining classes. The conditional proof combines this
exhaustive computational coverage with the reverse-permutation lower bound.
The upper bound remains conditional: no TD18 execution record or historical
joint-2 count supplies TD20 coverage. Every exit criterion in
\nolinkurl{docs/RESEARCH_PLAN.md} requires its own checked artifact.
\end{abstract}
\section{Pairs, moves, fixed symbols, and premises}
Let $\TD(n)$ be the maximum transposition distance in $S_n$, and set $N=n+1$. Let a cyclic-target pair be $(\omega,\beta)$ with even algebraic
permutation $\omega$, current $N$-cycle $\beta$, and target $\rho=\omega\beta$
also an $N$-cycle. Composition is from right to left. A transposition is an
applicable $3$-cycle $\tau$ ordered around the current cycle, acting as
$(\omega,\beta)\mapsto(\omega\tau^{-1},\tau\beta)$. Reserve $2$-cycle for an
algebraic cycle of length two. Define
$F(\omega)=\{x:\omega(x)=x\}$, $f(\omega)=|F(\omega)|$, and the gain
\[
g_\tau(\omega)=f(\omega\tau^{-1})-f(\omega).
\]
Gain is the net increase in fixed symbols. It equals the number of newly fixed
symbols when the input has no fixed points or the move preserves all existing
fixed points. Fixed points are first-class data: contract them from both cycles
before using a fixed-point-free generator or move test. Full-cycle move tests
instead retain existing fixed symbols explicitly and compute the net gain.
Contraction preserves distance
by the shared framework~\cite{framework},
\nolinkurl{prop:contraction-preserves-distance}.

A cycle on support $S$ is unoriented when it is the inverse of the current
cycle restricted to $S$; otherwise it is oriented. Define the $3$-norm by
$\kappa=(N-c_{\rm odd}(\omega))/2$, where $c_{\rm odd}$ includes fixed points.
A qualifying $2$-move increases that count by two and creates a fixed point.
The active rule does not require every first move to be qualifying and permits
first gain zero. It therefore imposes no unjustified $\kappa\leq7$ restriction
on the new endpoint inventory.

The established results $\TD(16)=9$ and $\TD(18)=10$ use the same
computational methodology of exhaustive class generation, intrinsic sorting
certificates, and independent regeneration and replay. Both are explicit
completed premises here, together with the established smaller diameters.
The shared-framework result is cited in~\cite{framework}; the TD18 reference
emission and separate-machine full-scope verification completed on
16 November 2026, with exit zero and all 1,349,910,890 reconstructed TD18
root classes checked~\cite{td18-reference}. Those execution records belong
to the established smaller result. The TD20 upper bound still requires its
own complete checked artifacts.
The insertion bound $\TD(n+1)\leq\TD(n)+1$ gives $\TD(19)\leq11$: after sorting the permutation with one symbol deleted, one transposition inserts that symbol at its target position. Thus a root with an existing fixed symbol requires no new diameter premise.
A known-diameter closure at a contracted domain of $m$ symbols is valid with
remaining budget $b$ exactly when $\TD(m-1)\leq b$ (distance zero for $m\leq1$).
A root with an existing fixed point closes using the established smaller bound.
The reverse root is separately closed by Eriksson et al.'s formula
$\lceil 21/2\rceil=11$~\cite{eriksson}.

\section{Joint selection on both endpoints}
Sorting distance on cycles is symmetric. Moving either endpoint by one edge
of its undirected transposition graph costs one in a triangle-inequality upper
bound. Interchanging endpoints is represented by $(\omega^{-1},\rho)$ and
costs nothing. This operation is used inside the argument; it is not silently
added to extended-toric class equivalence. Since
$F(\omega^{-1})=F(\omega)$, interchange preserves the fixed symbols; the same
net-gain definition applies to a move on either endpoint after this interchange.

Lemma 5.1 of Eriksson et al.~\cite{eriksson} gives three bonds using two block
moves for a non-reverse permutation, permitting moves on either side. The
lemma is not asserted to be a literal two-move forward prefix preserving the
original target. Bonds correspond to algebraic fixed points. Test all choices,
not only one construction promised by the lemma:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Test immediate gains at least two on either endpoint. Contraction leaves
at most 19 symbols and $1+\TD(18)\leq 11$ closes the root.
\item Otherwise inspect every applicable first transposition, of gain zero or
one, on either endpoint. A second move must then gain at least three or at least
two respectively to produce three fixed symbols. Two moves producing at least
four fixed symbols close by $2+\TD(16)\leq 11$.
\item For every result with exactly three fixed symbols, contract them and test
every third move of gain at least two, on either endpoint. Gain three leaves at
most 15 symbols, closing by $3+\TD(14)\leq 11$. Gain two
reaches a 16-symbol obligation with 8 moves remaining.
\item Retain every root without any such route. Only the explicitly cited,
budget-valid support patterns below may clear oriented retained roots. Every
other retained root requires a result with budget 11.
\end{enumerate}

A transposition changes only three images. Every previously fixed symbol $x$
in its support is destroyed, since injectivity and $\omega(x)=x$ imply
$\omega(\tau^{-1}(x))\ne x$. Let $d$ count these destroyed fixed symbols.
At most $3-d$ other support symbols become newly fixed, so the net gain is
at most $3-2d$. A move of gain at least two therefore preserves every existing
fixed symbol. In particular, contracting fixed points loses no eligible
support for the required second and third moves.

On a fixed-point-free input, every transposition of gain at least two is
supported, up to cyclic writing, on $(x,\omega(x),\omega^2(x))$, in current
cyclic order. Indeed, for $\tau=(a\ b\ c)$, its changed images fix $a,b,c$
exactly when $\omega(c)=a$, $\omega(a)=b$, $\omega(b)=c$, respectively.
Any two of these equalities give the stated cyclic writing, and the converse
follows from the same image calculation. This test includes even-length cycles.
First moves are enumerated over every applicable three-symbol support,
including supports meeting different algebraic cycles. Totally unoriented
roots, including all-odd roots, still enter gain-zero first-move tests.

\section{Endpoint cover and direct root inventory}
The endpoint cover consists of every even partition of 16 into parts at
least two and every orientation annotation, with algebraic $2$-cycles always
unoriented. A length-$k$ multiplicity $m_k$ contributes $m_k+1$ choices for
$k\geq3$. The complete annotation count is 143. The precondition is fixed-point
freedom, after contracting all five created fixed symbols. Every class must
have an 8-move witness or valid diameter closure, or enter final inverse
coverage. No predicted sorting-failure count is an input.

An endpoint with an oriented $3$-cycle clears that cycle in one move by
\cite{framework}, \nolinkurl{cor:oriented-three-cycles}, and closes whenever
$1+\TD(12)\leq 8$. A whole endpoint domain already satisfying
$\TD(15)\leq 8$ would be elementary in its entirety; the current diameter table does not close the whole 16-symbol domain within eight moves.

The structural inventory records all 143 annotations. Current-route class counts
must be regenerated independently in both modules before publication. They are
obligation counts, not sorting failures.

\subsection{Elementary closure of final inverse families}
\label{sec:inverse-families}
The following optimization changes which final roots are materialized, not
the sets $E,A,H,B,C$, the two inverse branches, or the budget-ten search rule.
It applies the first elementary closure of the joint-selection argument to
overlap introduced by the inverse cover.

\begin{lemma}[A surviving move on the untouched endpoint]
\label{lem:inverse-family}
Let $(\Omega,\beta)$ be a valid fixed-point-free source on a set $X$ of
$m\in\{20,21\}$ symbols, let $g=21-m$, let $\rho=\Omega\beta$, and set
$v=\Omega^{-1}$. For an old symbol $x\in X$, define $y=v(x)$ and $z=v(y)$.
Suppose $x,y,z$ are distinct and occur in cyclic order $(x,y,z)$ in $\rho$.
Restore $g$ fixed symbols in any cyclic gaps, extend $\Omega$ by the identity
on those symbols, and consider an applicable inverse transposition $\tau$
with support $S$ containing every restored symbol. If
$S\cap\{y,z\}=\varnothing$, then every valid fixed-point-free resulting
21-symbol parent has transposition distance at most eleven.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\bar\Omega,\bar\beta,\bar\rho=\bar\Omega\bar\beta$ for the restored
pair and its target. Inserting a fixed symbol $t$ into a current edge
$a\mapsto b$ replaces that edge by $a\mapsto t\mapsto b$. The corresponding
target edge $a\mapsto\Omega(b)$ becomes
$a\mapsto t\mapsto\Omega(b)$. Hence both restored cycles retain the
relative cyclic order of all old symbols. This also proves the assertion for
successive insertions, although this final wave restores at most one symbol.

The inverse parent is
$(\bar\Omega\tau^{-1},\tau\bar\beta)$, with unchanged target $\bar\rho$.
After interchanging endpoints, its algebraic permutation is
$q=\tau\bar\Omega^{-1}$ and its current cycle is $\bar\rho$. Since $\tau$
fixes $y$ and $z$, one has $q(x)=y$ and $q(y)=z$. The transposition
$\mu=(x\ y\ z)$ is applicable around $\bar\rho$, and
$(q\mu^{-1})(y)=q(x)=y$ and $(q\mu^{-1})(z)=q(y)=z$. The parent has no
fixed points, so this creates at least two fixed symbols. Contracting them
leaves at most nineteen cyclic symbols, and
$1+\TD(18)=11$ proves the bound. It is not necessary for $\tau$ to fix $x$.
Endpoint interchange costs nothing in the symmetric distance, so this is
also a bound for the original parent.
\end{proof}

For a current-endpoint inverse, define the \emph{witness masks} to be all
two-element sets $\{v(x),v^2(x)\}$ satisfying the distinctness and cyclic-order
conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse-family}. For a target-endpoint inverse,
interchange the source endpoints first: the masks use $v=\Omega$ and the
cyclic order $\beta$. This is a separate inverse branch, not a new class
equivalence. All masks contain old symbols only.

Let $G$ be the graph on $X$ whose edges are the witness masks. A final inverse
support has exactly $3-g$ old symbols. If those symbols do not meet every
edge of $G$, Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse-family} closes all its valid parents over
all restored gaps. Therefore only vertex covers of $G$ of size $3-g$ need
explicit expansion. If no cover of size at most $3-g$ exists, the entire
source-endpoint family closes. To decide this, select an uncovered edge and
branch on its two endpoints; the depth is at most $3-g\leq3$. Each edge must
be met, so the branches cover every possibility. A cover of smaller size can
be enlarged to the required size because $|X|\geq3-g$. Alternatively, inspect
every old support once and retain it exactly when it meets all masks.
These are sufficient closure tests: surviving supports may close in other
ways and are never declared hard merely by passing this filter.

The checker may independently decide the whole-family condition by connected
components. Each mask is an edge between consecutive symbols in a cycle of
$v$, so $G$ is a disjoint union of paths, cycles and isolated vertices; its
nonzero degrees are one or two. A path on $L$ vertices has minimum cover
$\lfloor L/2\rfloor$: disjoint alternating edges give that lower bound and
alternating vertices attain it. A cycle on $L$ vertices has minimum
cover $\lceil L/2\rceil$: each vertex covers at most two of its $L$ edges,
and an alternating selection, with one extra vertex for odd $L$, attains the
bound. Isolated vertices contribute zero, and disconnected components add.
The implementation checks the component degrees and endpoint counts before
using these formulas; an unexpected graph is an error, not a closure.

Every retained support is expanded through every gap and checked by actual
forward gain and pair replay. For every valid fixed-point-free parent, test
all consecutive algebraic triples on each endpoint. The earlier
consecutive-triple characterization makes this an exact test for membership
in $D_{11}$. A successful test checks applicability and the created fixed
symbols, then uses $1+\TD(18)\leq11$; all other parents enter the canonical
disk-backed set $R$.

\begin{proposition}[Completeness of the filtered final inventory]
\label{prop:filtered-final-inventory}
The family filter followed by the exact two-sided immediate-gain test
generates precisely $R=P\setminus D_{11}$ after class deduplication. Checking
every class in $R$ within eleven moves discharges every class in $P$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every skipped support family lies in $D_{11}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse-family}, and every individually skipped parent lies in
$D_{11}$ by its applicable transposition and fixed-symbol test. Thus no
member of $P\setminus D_{11}$ is removed from the complete inverse cover.
Every emitted parent is in $P$ by forward replay and outside $D_{11}$ by the
exact two-sided test. These tests and the existence of a closing move are
invariant under the relabelings and twisted dihedral action defining the
valid-pair class, so canonical deduplication gives exactly $R$. Classes in
$P\cap D_{11}$ have the established elementary bound; the assumed checks of
$R$ cover the rest.
\end{proof}

The checker independently regenerates every source in $C$, validates both
source cycles and fixed-point freedom, reconstructs the masks from the
actual algebraic images and opposite cyclic order, and independently checks
the support-cover condition. It covers both inverse endpoints and both
domain-dependent gains. It uses the universal lemma only for those families;
every other accepted parent retains exact inverse replay and the two-sided
immediate-gain check before canonicalization. Every class in its independently
generated set $R$ requires a persisted intrinsic result replayed at budget
eleven. No per-source family certificate or obstruction marker is a proof
input: these are directly verified elementary closures, just as in initial
root generation. Missing source work or a missing result in $R$ prevents
completion. Small-domain validation compares this filtered set, including
class multiplicities before deduplication, with complete unfiltered inverse
enumeration followed by an independent arbitrary-support closure oracle.


Within one sorting search, the target $\rho$ is fixed, so the full algebraic
permutation $\omega$ determines the current cycle
$\beta=\omega^{-1}\rho$; choosing another starting position of that cycle
does not change its legal supports. A bounded search cache may therefore
retain the least spent depth for each exact $\omega$. A shallower occurrence
has at least as much remaining obligation budget and exploration depth.
The immediate-inverse restriction only omits an edge back to an already visited
parent: deleting the adjacent inverse pair preserves a successful continuation.
Thus exact-state depth dominance preserves the existence of a permitted
certificate. Hash collisions are compared using the complete key; an exhausted
cache probe falls back to ordinary exploration. The cache is cleared between
inputs and supplies no proof discharge by itself.

\subsection{The zero-move allowance and capped all-odd searches}
Here a \emph{zero move} preserves the number of odd-length cycles; this is
distinct from a move of fixed-symbol gain zero. Let
$h=\ell_3(\omega)=(N-c_{\rm odd}(\omega))/2$, including fixed points.
This is the minimum number of unrestricted algebraic $3$-cycles in a
factorization of $\omega$~\cite[Proposition 3.1]{muhle-nadeau}, hence a
lower bound for sorting transpositions. In a complete sorting of length $L$
using only zero moves and moves increasing $c_{\rm odd}$ by two, the number
of zero moves is $L-h$. Thus a budget $B$ permits at most $B-h$ zero moves.
The ordinary rank lower-bound test already enforces this allowance.

Let $D(m)$ denote the known diameter for a contracted domain of $m$ symbols,
with an unknown entry interpreted as $+\infty$. Define
\[
 R=\max\bigl(\{0\}\cup
 \{r\geq1:\text{ some }m\text{ satisfies }2r+1\leq m\leq N,
                                      \ D(m)\leq r\}\bigr).
\]
Set $R=0$ when diameter closures are disabled. The rank zero case represents
identity. For the current table and $N\geq3$, $R=1$ when closures are enabled.

\begin{lemma}[Capped all-odd search with no zero-move allowance]
At a search node, let $b$ be its remaining full sorting budget and $k$ its
remaining exploration depth. Suppose every cycle of $\omega$ has odd length
and $h=b$. If $h>k+R$, no explored continuation can reach identity or a
diameter closure within those limits.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After $t$ permitted moves containing $z$ zero moves, the residual rank is
$r=h-t+z$, while the remaining budget is $h-t$. If $z>0$, the rank lower
bound excludes both completion and a sound diameter closure, including one
whose unsearched suffix uses unrestricted sorting moves. Any potentially
successful prefix therefore consists entirely of rank-decreasing moves.
An all-odd permutation remains all odd under such moves
by~\cite[Corollary 3.3 and Remark 3.5]{muhle-nadeau}.
Contract its fixed points. If $r>0$ and $c$ nontrivial cycles remain, its
domain size is $m=2r+c\geq2r+1$. A diameter closure with remaining budget
$r$ consequently requires $r\leq R$. But $t\leq k$ implies
$r=h-t\geq h-k>R\geq0$, excluding both a closure and identity.
\end{proof}

CUDA derives $R$ from the actual supplied diameter table and applies the
strict inequality only when the rank equals the remaining full budget.
This rejects a subtree as unresolved; it never discharges an obligation or
omits any required inverse reconstruction.

\section{Certificates, persistence, and proof of the diameter}
Every persisted set has a version, deterministic keys, explicit budgets,
domains and counts, and rejects missing data. Large inverse sets are disk-backed;
asynchronous certificate persistence uses bounded queues. A witness proves an
upper bound only for its represented class. Sorting uses separate CPU and
GPU worker pools communicating through a versioned disk queue. Full-search
emission transfers budget-nine GPU misses to a CPU inverse pool through a
second queue; every transferred case is processed before sealing the next wave. Active
residual-expansion emission instead generates the complete budget-ten wave
and covers the final inverse wave on CPU, using the elementary families in
Section~\ref{sec:inverse-families} and no budget-nine or budget-ten sorting queues. Every source
class is accounted for and all generation and persistence workers join before
the next wave is ready for processing. Both modes use the filtered final
inverse wave. Production flushes the proof-scoped certificate writer before
publishing a downstream completed checkpoint: results used to omit upstream
sources must persist before that downstream subset can be resumed. A failed
flush prevents checkpoint publication. After budget-ten generation completes,
a key-only scan counts the distinct classes through RocksDB's merged
logical keys and records the exact count in the emitter checkpoint
before final inverse generation begins. Parallel iterators use disjoint
half-open byte ranges on a shared snapshot: each split preserves the union
of the parent range, so summing the completed range counts gives the exact
logical-key inventory. Approximate byte sizes guide scheduling only.
Canonical persisted keys already
identify distinct classes, so this scan neither decodes or validates states
nor performs a further in-memory deduplication or advisory search. Legacy
version-three checkpoints with a deferred count remain readable for completed
residual generation; resume counts that stored set and atomically upgrades
the checkpoint before final generation. The source inventory and independent
derivation of the second-level residuals are unchanged by final filtering.
The filtered final-root checkpoint uses version four, retains either
\nolinkurl{mode=FULL_SEARCH} or \nolinkurl{mode=RESIDUAL_EXPANSION}, and records
\nolinkurl{rootInventory=ELEMENTARY_RESIDUALS}; its root count means $|R|$,
not $|P|$. A completed historical after-two or after-one
residual-expansion source may be explicitly adopted because these source sets
are unchanged, after domain, budget, mode and inventory validation. Historical
complete-root checkpoints cannot be adopted as filtered-root checkpoints.
Neither full-search checkpoints nor TD18 checkpoints can supply filtered
residual-expansion input merely because their numerical counts coincide.
Interrupted queues are regenerated from their completed
source wave. Final-root sorting retains the CPU/GPU pools and witness replay.
Inverse outputs use deterministic writer shards with bounded queues. Shards
sort distinct keys in unsigned byte order, build SST files independently,
and ingest them with global sequence numbers enabled. Every repeated key
has the same empty value, so overlapping tables represent one set entry.
The independent checker additionally performs exact persisted-membership
lookups in batches of at most $4096$ keys before building an SST, retaining
only absent keys. Equal keys belong to one serial writer and each ingestion
completes before that writer's next lookup; hence this filtering removes only
entries already in the same set. Nonempty stored values, failed lookups and

Bloom filters accelerate these exact lookups without determining membership.
After a complete source scan and successful sealing of its successor, the

accumulation of repeated physical entries and completed scratch waves.
Producer buffers retain output across successive callbacks. After every

the exact inventory is checked; a generation or persistence failure prevents

Final expansion reports the number $X$ of completed expanded source classes

Canonical key construction need not decode a canonical pair first. In the
canonical fixed-content word, let $p_1<\cdots<p_k$ be the occurrences of an
unoriented color. Its algebraic images are $p_i\mapsto p_{i-1}$, with indices
read cyclically. For an oriented color, the occurrence of rank $r$ maps to the
occurrence of rank $r+1$, also cyclically. These are exactly the decoding rules,
so packing those images directly gives the same five-bit key. Singleton colors
retain fixed points for certificate lookup; reconstruction-state admission
requires them to have been contracted. Auxiliary standardization and the twisted
dihedral action are unchanged. A persisted normalized input is reused for replay
only if its actual images equal the computed canonical key; no stored key alone
confers canonicality. Every certificate hit is replayed, and its derived budget
is checked against both the project maximum and the requested budget. Reusing
that derived budget avoids a duplicate replay without weakening either check.

restored domain: $(\omega\tau^{-1})(\tau\beta)=\omega\beta$, and swapping
endpoints has target $\omega^{-1}\rho=\beta$. Owned objects have fresh arrays;
every explicitly constructed parent still passes its actual forward gain and
pair replay. Family exclusions additionally require the elementary closure
lemma; target reuse alone removes no support or gap.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional transposition diameter]
\label{thm:td20}
Under the established smaller-diameter premises stated above and the checked
computational coverage specified in \nolinkurl{docs/RESEARCH_PLAN.md},
$\TD(20)=11$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A root with a fixed symbol contracts to a known smaller domain, and the reverse
root has distance eleven by the cited reverse formula. For every other root, joint
selection either closes it directly by a smaller diameter, reaches a 16-symbol
endpoint with eight moves remaining, or leaves a direct root obligation.
The complete 83-partition root inventory and 143 annotated endpoint types cover
these cases. The computational coverage discharges every direct root within eleven
moves and every endpoint within eight moves or through its complete three-wave
inverse reconstruction. Both inverse branches and both endpoints are covered.
Proposition~\ref{prop:filtered-final-inventory} discharges the elementary
final parents and reduces the remaining requirement to $R$. The independent
checker regenerates every source and residual obligation, verifies the
elementary closures, and replays the persisted results at their actual
budgets. Thus every root has distance at most eleven.
The reverse root supplies the matching lower bound, giving $\TD(20)=11$.
\end{proof}

The theorem uses checked computational coverage as well as the established
mathematical premises. Count totals, partial runs, and software tests alone do
not supply that coverage. \nolinkurl{docs/RESEARCH_PLAN.md} enumerates the
required evidence. The upper bound is pending until every criterion has a
checked artifact. \nolinkurl{docs/REFERENCE_SIMULATION.md} specifies the
execution record to complete for TD20; TD18 timings and results are not TD20
artifacts.
